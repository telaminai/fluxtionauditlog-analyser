WHY THIS NOTE EXISTS

The cherry-pick of dd36bc5 onto feature/compiler_diagnostics is not a replay of one commit onto an
unrelated base. Both branches implemented GraphML edge metadata, independently, within an hour of
each other, in the same file. `9402fe7` ("Slice 4: the additive edge keys, and the edge descriptor
they needed first", 10:21) landed on the feature branch; `dd36bc5` (10:5x) went to the handoff
branch. Neither session could see the other's work.

They agree on the diagnosis and disagree on three answers. A textual conflict resolution will pick
one of each silently, and two of the three are decisions someone has already made deliberately.

RECOMMENDED RESOLUTION — take parts, do not replay

`EdgeReference` is the better carrier and should win. It captures the relationship in the model,
where the compiler decides what a reference IS. dd36bc5's walker re-derives it by reflection
afterwards, which is the defect BOTH reviews of that spike named, and which dd36bc5's own spec names
as the condition that must be met before its default can be flipped on. If EdgeReference is
authoritative, that condition is already satisfied on the feature branch — which is the happiest
possible outcome of the collision.

So the suggested shape of the merged result:

  KEEP from 9402fe7      the EdgeReference model capture and its derivation
  TAKE from dd36bc5      the metadata mode toggle and the default-OFF decision;
                         the node-level and graph-level keys;
                         handler-edge facts (method, filter value, filter type);
                         the edgedefault correction (independent defect fix, see below);
                         deterministic ordering of emitted relationships and merged values;
                         the key manifest and its two-way conformance test;
                         the acceptance tests, which encode the criteria rather than describing them
  DECIDE                 the three items below

THREE DECISIONS THAT ARE NOT CONFLICT-RESOLUTION CALLS

D1 · refKind or referenceKind. One name ships. This is open question 4 in
`docs/specs/meta-data-improvements/README.md` (inside dd36bc5). The analyser's ask UP-FLX-28 asked
for `refKind`; the compiler-diagnostics README used `fluxtion.referenceKind`. Whichever wins, the
key manifest and the conformance test must be updated with it, or the test will fail — which is the
test doing its job.

D2 · Unconditional or opt-in. The owner chose default OFF on 2026-08-31, on the acceptance point
that property-absent, blank and unrecognised must all mean OFF, and that the vocabulary should not
change any artefact until a consumer opts in. 9402fe7 emits unconditionally. Cherry-picking dd36bc5
on top without reconciling this yields a toggle governing one emission path while another emits
beside it regardless — the worst of both. If EdgeReference makes the facts authoritative, the
argument for OFF weakens and flipping the default becomes a live option; that is a decision to take
explicitly, not by merge order.

D3 · Edge shape. Joined values on one edge, or one edge per relationship. dd36bc5 supports both and
defaults to neither being forced: AGGREGATED is the joined form and its edge list is PROVEN
identical to the pre-vocabulary document — same pairs, same order — by an acceptance test. That
makes 9402fe7's choice available as a mode rather than a fork, if that is wanted.

THE EDGEDEFAULT FIX IS INDEPENDENT

`edgedefault` has always said "undirected" for a directed dependency graph, because the test is
`g instanceof SimpleGraph` and SimpleDirectedGraph is not a subclass of it. That is a defect, not
vocabulary, and it is not governed by any toggle in dd36bc5. It can ship on its own regardless of how
D1-D3 land. The analyser is unaffected either way — it never reads the attribute, and its own parser
javadoc already calls today's value misleading — but a conformant reader loses dispatch direction.

WHAT THE TESTS ARE FOR, AND TWO THAT EARNED THEIR PLACE

dd36bc5 carries 14 acceptance tests against a document the COMPILER produced, parsed with a general
XML parser rather than the emitter's own code. Two failed on first run, before the defects were
known:

* the manifest conformance check failed because a key was in the emitter and not in the published
  manifest — drift caught before it shipped;
* the byte-stability check passed in isolation and FAILED in the full suite: Class.getMethods() has
  no defined order and a merged value list came out reordered between runs. Two builds of one graph
  would have told a consumer about a change that did not happen. Emission is now sorted.

Both checks are worth keeping through the merge whichever way D1-D3 go. The second one is the reason
to be careful with any reflection-ordered emission that survives.

VERIFIED / NOT VERIFIED

Verified: full compiler suite on dd36bc5, 3428 tests, zero failures. The analyser's own parser reads
the PARALLEL output with identical nodes, kinds, class names and adjacency; only its edge count
changes (11 to 14 on the probe graph), and its layer assignment is unaffected because in-degree is
incremented and decremented once per duplicate.

Not verified: crossing-reduction / barycentre ordering under duplicate edges; any GraphML consumer
other than the analyser; and the merged behaviour of the two implementations, which does not exist
yet — everything above about the combined result is a recommendation, not a measurement.
