  This adds a FIFTEENTH VERB, `handoff`. The surface was deliberately pinned at fourteen — close, project,
  discover and analysis were each folded into `open` instead, and four tests held the number. I moved the
  number. My reasoning: the rule those tests guard is that the surface does not grow FOR A CONCEPT AN
  EXISTING VERB ALREADY NAMES. Those four are lifecycle acts, so they belong on `open`. Posture and the
  selector's record are not a lifecycle act, and `context` cannot take a write because it must stay
  read-only (M42's loopback probe depends on that). Overloading either is one name with two meanings — the
  move D-T4 refused for `rowWhen`. If the owner disagrees, the alternative is `open {posture, handoff}`:
  about forty lines to move, and the four tests go back to 14. Each updated test states this in its message.

1. WHAT THE TRACKER LINE MEANT, AND WHAT I TOOK IT TO REQUIRE
================================================================================
M48.7 was one sentence. Read against the spec it is two things: (R7 revised) the mode selector's handoff
record is "shared context, not a view: one piece of state, rendered for the human, exposed to the LLM
through context, and writable by either"; and (R10) "either party may set the analyser's posture … through
MCP or the UI, and derivation is only the fallback".

Four choices, each taken FROM the specs rather than invented:
  a. THE ANALYSER NEVER RUNS THE SELECTOR. R6 says "the analyser calls it"; the spec's own later Correction
     ("the analyser is the renderer, not the intelligence") supersedes that. Whoever ran the selector
     PLACES its record. This also keeps D-AI4 (nothing on the AI menu runs anything) intact.
  b. SESSION STATE, NOTHING PERSISTED. The canvas spec: the profile "grows only through reviewed key
     families" and "model writes default to reversible curation". Writing a chosen convention back to the
     profile (R9) is a new key family and is NOT in this slice — recorded as not built, on purpose.
  c. THE CANVAS'S SEVEN WRITE RULES, APPLIED: visible (one state, two renderings) · typed (a closed record
     shape; an unknown field is REFUSED, not dropped) · attributed (who, when) · scoped (cleared at a
     project transition) · reversible (`clear`) · fail-closed (a malformed record is refused WHOLE, and a
     posture sent in the same call is not applied) · bounded (200 items, 200 chars, one line each).
  d. DERIVATION IS SIMPLER THAN R10's SENTENCE. "A buildable project" is unknowable without running a
     build, so: a project open reads as authoring, otherwise research — and the payload SAYS it is a guess.

4. WHAT A REVIEWER SHOULD ATTACK
================================================================================
· THE FIFTEENTH VERB (above). It is the owner's call more than a reviewer's, but say what you think.
· "REFUSE AN UNKNOWN FIELD" vs the house habit of FORWARD TOLERANCE (M38.7 preserves unknown keys; the
  format spec tolerates unknown record fields). I chose refusal because this is an agent WRITING to a
  surface a person reads — a silently dropped field is a fact the agent believes is on the canvas and is
  not. But when the selector's record gains a field, every older analyser will refuse the whole record.
  The alternative is accept-and-name-ignored, as the verbs do for params (M26.4). I am not certain I chose
  right; it is a one-line change either way.
· POSTURE IS CLEARED AT A PROJECT BOUNDARY. Arguable: posture is the person's intent for the sitting, and
  a project switch may not change it. I cleared it because the derivation changes at exactly that moment
  and a stale SET posture would then contradict what is open with nobody having said so recently.
· NOTHING CONSUMES POSTURE YET. It is stated, settable and rendered; no behaviour branches on it. That is
  deliberate (R10 asks for the state, and M48.10 is the consumer) but it means this slice is unfalsifiable
  in use until something reads it.
· The AI menu's source-text tests (AiMenuTest) still pass; check my additions did not merely avoid its
  patterns — e.g. the listeners call applyHandoffFromMenu / placeHandoffRecordFromFile, which live outside
  the menu body the test reads.
