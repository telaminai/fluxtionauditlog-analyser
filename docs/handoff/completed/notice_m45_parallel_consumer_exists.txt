================================================================================
1 · WHAT THE ANALYSER NOW DOES WITH A PARALLEL FILE
================================================================================
Reads it — `GraphVocabulary` carries mode, `metaVersion` and a trust decision, and node/edge
`fluxtion.*` facts travel on the model.

Renders it — one line per vertex PAIR, while the model keeps every relationship. That was the specific
thing "reading" did not cover: our layout and canvas drew straight from the edge list with no
deduplication, so a doubled pair produced two identical polylines painted over each other and counted
the pair twice as a layering constraint. Fixed, and the fix is mutation-checked.

Distinguishes them — a pair's relationships stay separable by `(source, target, refKind,
referenceField)`, because since your per-relationship propagation change two edges on one pair can
legitimately disagree about `propagates`, and that disagreement is the fact worth showing.

Uses a fact it previously could not establish — `fluxtion.auditCapable` now reaches the coverage
denominator. The gain is not accuracy over reading source; it is that **it needs no source at all**,
and missing source is the normal case for a log someone else produced, where our text check correctly
but unhelpfully returns UNKNOWN and keeps the node counted.

Refuses `AGGREGATED` — see §3.

Pins `topologicalRank` against the dispatch order we already know rather than trusting it, for the
reason your own §8 gave.

================================================================================
2 · THE THING YOU CANNOT SEE FROM YOUR SIDE — FLIPPING HAS A COST FOR RELEASED ANALYSERS
================================================================================
**Every analyser released before today draws a PARALLEL file wrong.** Not broken — adjacency is
set-based and survives — but a doubled pair renders as a heavier arrow rather than two relationships,
and `edgeCount()` inflates. On this repo's own graph that is 43 edges becoming 44.

The fix is in `main` and is **not in v1.11.0**, the current release. So:

  · flip the default before an analyser carrying M45.5 ships, and every existing install renders
    slightly wrong on every new graph — quietly, because nothing tells the user;
  · flip it after, and the cost is only borne by people who choose not to upgrade.

We are not asking you to wait — it is a cosmetic regression, not a correctness one, and you may
reasonably decide it is worth it. **But it should be a decision rather than a surprise**, and it is the
kind of thing only the downstream side can tell you. If it helps, say when you intend to flip and we
will line a release up against it.

================================================================================
3 · AGGREGATED HAS NO CONSUMER, AND WE ARE THE CONSUMER IT WAS FOR
================================================================================
We refuse it, deliberately and permanently — D-V1 in `spec-graphml-vocabulary-consumption.md`. Your own
javadoc is what decided it: *"the lists are sets, not index-aligned tuples"*. A merged filter value
cannot be matched back to the handler it belongs to, so a reader that rendered one would be wrong
without being able to detect it. Since propagation became per-relationship it is worse than when we
first assessed it: aggregation now loses a fact rather than blurring one, because two relationships on
a pair can genuinely disagree.

`meta-data-improvements` positions AGGREGATED as the compatible shape for exactly the consumer that has
now decided not to use it. Worth either repositioning it — it is a fine shape for a reader that wants
presence and counts, not per-relationship facts — or dropping it. Not a finding, just information you
should have before maintaining a bridge nobody crosses.
