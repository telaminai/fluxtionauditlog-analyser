================================================================================
1 · THE ISSUE MAPPING — two rows wrong, and the dangerous one was unflagged
================================================================================
They wrote: *"If one is wrong, the fix is a wrong issue link, which is worse than none."* Agreed, and that
is exactly what happened twice.

**A · "doc anchors (Slice 7) → #22 / UP-FLX-35" is WRONG.** This row was NOT flagged as judgement.

  * Doc anchors — a stable URL per rejection code, `fluxtion.dev/errors/FLX-1001` — is **UP-SHARED-02**,
    and it is **filed as issue #8**, alongside UP-FLX-01.
  * **#22 / UP-FLX-35** is something else entirely: *"the audit contract is undocumented — usage is shown,
    `EventLogSource` is never named"*. It is about how a node records values, not about error-code URLs.

An implementer following the table to Slice 7 lands on an issue that says nothing about doc anchors, and
the issue that does — #8, already open, already carrying the argument that a stable URL is what makes
semantics reachable *at the moment of failure* — goes uncited.

**B · "`fluxtion.callbackKinds` / `FLX-1008` → #21 / UP-FLX-34" is muddled.** They flagged this one, and
were right to.

  * `callbackKinds` (lifecycle/annotation facts — `@AfterEvent`, `@Initialise`, `@Start`,
    `@AfterTrigger`) is **UP-FLX-14, filed as #12** — *"GraphML: carry lifecycle/annotation facts"*.
  * **#21 / UP-FLX-34** is *"declare whether a node can log (`EventLogSource`)"* — audit capability.
  * `FLX-1008` is a third thing again: `auditLog` used in a graph built without `addEventAudit`.

Three facts in one row. Split it: `callbackKinds` → #12, audit capability → #21, and `FLX-1008` is the
diagnostic that neither GraphML key covers.

**C · One direction of the check is missing, and it has already cost a citation.** The table asks *"does
every code have an issue?"* It does not ask *"does every filed issue have a code?"* — and
`fluxtion.originCapable` is the proof: *"nodes that can raise reentrant events if statically known"* is
the static half of **UP-FLX-10, filed as #10** (*mark a re-dispatched record as internally caused*), which
the analyser calls its single biggest win. The spec has the key; the table cites nothing.

**Everything else in the table I checked resolves correctly**, including the four that are load-bearing:
FLX-1001→#19, FLX-1020→#14, FLX-1021→#15, FLX-1030→#17, FLX-1031→#18, Spring→#20.

================================================================================
2 · "THREE ASKS NOT COVERED" — two confirmed absent, one needs narrowing
================================================================================
They worried a concept might hide under vocabulary they did not guess. I grepped their spec for the
words they tried **and six they did not** (`propagate`, `FilterType`, `conditional`, `scaffold`,
`NoTrigger`, `push`). Result:

  * **UP-FLX-29 (mark framework-generated nodes) — ABSENT, confirmed.** "framework" occurs twice, both in
    prose about not inferring semantics, never as a key. `fluxtion.originCapable` is about reentrancy, not
    scaffolding, so it does not quietly cover it. Their "cheapest big win" framing is the analyser's and I
    stand behind it: without this, every node count, coverage figure and topology view silently includes
    framework plumbing.
  * **UP-FLX-31 (filter value and match strategy) — ABSENT, confirmed.** `filter`, `FilterType` and
    `conditional` are all zero.
  * **UP-FLX-28 — "half absent" is right, but the half they identified is wrong.** The spec DOES carry
    push: `fluxtion.referenceKind` takes `TRIGGER | DATA | **PUSH**`, and `@PushReference` is named in the
    Slice acceptance. What is genuinely absent is **`propagates`** — and specifically the four distinct
    carriers of non-propagation I documented: `@ExportService(propagate=false)`,
    `@NoPropagateFunction`, `@OnEventHandler(propagate=false)` and `FilterType.matched`. None appears.
    So the correction is: *push is covered as a reference kind; whether an update CROSSES an edge is not.*

That distinction matters to the consumer for the reason their own document gives — `refKind` says what the
reference is, `propagates` says whether an update crosses it — and a reader needs both to separate
"did not run" from "could not have run".

3 · THE MODULE ANSWER — correct, and better evidence exists than the one cited
================================================================================
They argued from the dependency direction. Verified: `fluxtion-generator-core/pom.xml` declares
`fluxtion-builder`, so core → builder and a DTO in builder creates no cycle. I checked the reverse
direction too — builder's only Fluxtion dependencies are `fluxtion-builder-api` and `fluxtion-runtime`.

**But the decisive evidence is a comment in `fluxtion-builder/pom.xml` that the document does not cite:**

    fluxtion-builder is the distributed closed-source base that carries the generation model/DTO/SPI;
    fluxtion-generator-core (cloud-only) depends DOWN onto this module and is never shipped with builder.

So the module **already declares itself the home for the generation model, DTO and SPI**, and already
states that generator-core is cloud-only and never shipped. That is documented intent rather than an
inference from graph direction, and it closes open question 1 more firmly than the argument given. Quote
it — a maintainer reading their own comment back is harder to argue with than a dependency arrow.

================================================================================
4 · THE EXPENSIVE ACCEPTANCE — I would keep it, and I can say why from measurement
================================================================================
They expected someone to argue the fixture pack belongs later. I argue the opposite, from data rather than
taste.

**"A hand-written fixture proves only that the author can write JSON"** is precisely the failure this
project has hit repeatedly and recently. In the last two days: a packaging test injected a stub processor,
so *"the declared GraphML exists"* passed on a file the product never wrote; a bench fixture declared a
contract version that made a whole check unreachable, so a mutation passed and I nearly reported an
untested guard as sound. **Both were green suites over artefacts no user path produces.** A fixture pack
generated by running the compiler is the only thing that closes that class.

**And the one repair loop end to end is the only test of the actual claim.** Every unit test in the plan
verifies the compiler emits a diagnostic. None verifies anyone can act on one — and an unconsumable
diagnostic is the failure being fixed, restated in JSON. That sentence in their document is the best line
in it.

The cost is real and they are right to flag it. My view: gate Slice 2 with the fixture pack for `Ready`
codes only, and let the single repair loop follow Slice 2 rather than gate it — the loop needs a consumer,
and the consumer is cheaper to write once one real fixture exists.

**An offer rather than an opinion.** We have a harness that runs fresh, context-free agents against a real
generated project and records whether they recover in one step, whether they learn the *rule* or only the
trick that silenced the error, and what they came away believing. Pointed at a candidate message it answers
the question no unit test can: *is this followable by someone who does not already know the answer?* Two
agents, about twenty minutes per candidate. See
`docs/proposals/notes-for-the-compiler-diagnostics-work.md` in the analyser repo.

================================================================================
WHAT HELD
================================================================================
* The timing judgement is the document's strongest feature. "No `FLX-` code appears in any `.java` file"
  was checked rather than assumed, and reconciling two independently-written documents before either is
  implemented is exactly when it is cheap.
* Finding 3's two collisions are real and correctly scoped as *raise, do not decide*. `referenceKind` vs
  `refKind` is a published-surface naming choice, and the spec's own principle 4 (existing readers keep
  working) is precisely the promise a later rename breaks. Same for the fingerprint carrier: a consumer
  holding only a `.graphml` cannot reach a processor descriptor, so "both are defensible, they are not
  interchangeable" is the right framing.
* The discipline of deciding nothing that belongs to the compiler owner — no severities, no key names, no
  fingerprint carrier, four of five open questions untouched — is correct, and rarer than it should be.
