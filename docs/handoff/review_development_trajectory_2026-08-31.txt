ASSESSMENT — the trajectory of the compiler diagnostics / GraphML / Spring → LLM work
Scope: fluxtion-compiler feature/compiler_diagnostics through afad4ba, and this repo's M45
Written: 2026-08-31, by the compiler-side session, at the owner's request

DISCLOSURE: I am entangled in this work. I wrote the original GraphML spike, the
meta-data-improvements spec and the model-rationalisation proposal, and several improvements
described below are responses to my own reviews. I am therefore a poor judge of whether the direction
is right, and a reasonable judge of whether the work is CHECKABLE. That is the axis this document
sticks to.

VERDICT: Good trajectory, and the reason is specific — the verification loop has been internalised
rather than imposed. The risk has moved. It is no longer "is this the right design"; it is "will it
ship, and will anyone be able to tell it worked".

Slices 1–4 ran green at about seven minutes each. Slice 5 failed in **34 seconds**, and the run log

It is the same problem a reviewer hits locally: the branch pins `fluxtion.base.version` to
`1.0.14-SNAPSHOT`, those artefacts are on no published repository, and `main.yml` has no step that
builds the paired public repo. CI cannot resolve what a human has to install by hand.

**The loop is not missing — it fired correctly and immediately.** The signal was not picked up: two
hours and one review conversation passed without it being mentioned, including by me until I went
looking. That is the more tractable version of the problem and it has two acceptable answers:

* add a public-repo checkout-and-install step to `main.yml`, pinned to the paired commit
  (`fc8d776` on `telaminai/fluxtion` `origin/develop`); or
* accept the red for the duration of the pin and say so in the tracker, so the light means something
  again.

An ignored red light stops working as a light. This is the one item in this document with a deadline.

1 · WHAT IS GENUINELY GOOD, WITH EVIDENCE RATHER THAN IMPRESSION
================================================================================

**The review loop has been internalised.** The strongest evidence is not that review findings were
fixed — it is the three things that happened without a reviewer asking:

* `dbcbe17` — a self-caught defect where a rename made `topologicalRank` LOOK repaired while the
  accessor still returned the wrong list. Measured impact recorded in the commit: published rank was
  the reverse of dispatch across every push edge. Finding that in your own work, in the report that
  claimed to remove that defect class, is the hardest kind of catch;
* `ac231a8` — the Kryo id gate was mutation-verified: a registration was inserted and `NodeDto`
  watched moving from id 10 to 11, so the test is known to fail for the reason it claims;
* the tracker carries **"Fingerprint | Emitted, contract NOT met"** against its own slice, naming four
  specific shortfalls. Self-reporting a gap before a reviewer finds it is the single best signal in
  the whole engagement.

**The evidence rules come from real failures, not from principle.** "A diagnostic test drives the
production classifier, not a factory with hand-built facts." "A claimed repair is applied to the same
producer fixture and must compile." "Determinism compares complete same-input bytes — no timestamp
stripping is allowed to manufacture a pass." That last rule closed a loophole MY OWN test used. Rules
written after being burned are worth several written in advance.

**The architecture converged the hard way, which is the durable way.** "Decide once, carry forward"
was not decreed: three defects in three subsystems — the FLX-1009 misclassification, the exporter
re-deriving relationships, the Spring rule reading the wrong representation — independently pointed at
it. It is now a gate with an authority table, and the owner's version improved on the proposal by
keeping GraphML-only facts off the remote DTO.

**Scope discipline held under delivery pressure.** `FLX-1002` stayed blocked rather than shipping a
breaking change dressed as a diagnostic. The metadata default stayed `OFF` through five slices that
would have been satisfying to switch on. This repo refused `AGGREGATED` with stated reasons rather
than taking the mode that looked compatible.

================================================================================
2 · THREE RISKS, IN THE ORDER I WOULD WORRY ABOUT THEM
================================================================================

**R1 · Velocity is outrunning verification.** Nine commits and ~2,400 lines in a day is not the
problem. The branch being unbuildable by anyone else for two hours without that registering is the
problem, and §0 is its instance. The fix is mechanical.

**R2 · Built-but-not-delivered keeps growing.** Twenty GraphML keys emitted, a public collector seam,
remote transport, five slices — and the metadata default is still `OFF`, the fingerprint's contract is
unmet by its own account, `eventAudit` is `planned`, the Spring vocabulary is partly implemented, and
the consumer milestone has not started. The release question raised earlier is still unanswered: what
an author actually gets today is better cycle messages and two logged warnings. That is real, and it
is a fraction of what has been built.

**R3 · Prose is outpacing executable contracts.** The written material is unusually good — but two
documents went stale within hours of being written (M45's SHA pin; my own integration note, which
carried two errors), and a "document ownership" table now exists because there is enough material to
need one. The manifest conformance test is the right pattern: a contract that RUNS and fails when the
emitter and the document disagree — which it did, on its first execution. More of that, less of the
other.

================================================================================
3 · THE MEASUREMENT THAT WOULD SETTLE IT
================================================================================

The `n=9` baseline is the only OUTPUT measure in this programme. It is honest about being
observational and hypothesis-generating, and it has not been re-run since the work intended to move
it. Everything shipped this week is an input.

The compiler's own acceptance questions are the right ones and only one is answered mechanically:

  1. does the diagnostic state the compiler rule rather than one favoured patch?          — not re-measured
  2. does its suggested repair compile when applied to the same failing graph?            — answered by tests
  3. can an author without prior context recover in one step and explain why?             — not re-measured

One held-out fresh-session run against the improved diagnostics would say more about whether this is
working than the next slice will. It is also the only evidence that would survive someone asking
"did the diagnostics programme pay for itself".
